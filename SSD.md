# The Sovereign Software Definition (SSD)

A Sovereign software license is a gratis and Source Available license, that doesn't just mean access to the source code. The distribution terms of Sovereign software grant the licensor sovereignty over their work but must comply with the following criteria. 

The rights and freedoms described apply to those whom the license makes eligible; this definition permits a license to define who is eligible, what they may use the software for, and whether derived works are allowed, among other restrictions.

A sovereign license defaults to open, but gives the licensor control over the licensed work. The licensor may grant or restrict permissions, specifically or generally. 

1. **Free Redistribution**

The license shall not restrict any eligible party from distributing the software in whole, in part or as a component of an aggregate software distribution containing programs from several different sources. The license shall not require a royalty or other fee for such distribution.

2. **Source Code**

The program must include source code, and must allow distribution in source code as well as compiled form. Where some form of a product is not distributed with source code, there must be means of obtaining the source code for no more than a reasonable reproduction cost, preferably downloading via the Internet without charge. 

3. **Derived Works**

The license may permit or forbid modifications and derived works, and may set whatever terms the licensor chooses for their distribution. Liberty remains with the licensor.

4. **Integrity of The Source Code**

The license may restrict source-code from being distributed in modified form. The license may require derived works to carry a different name or version number from the original software.

5. **Eligible Licensees**

The license may declare which persons, groups, entities or organizational forms are eligible to exercise the rights it grants, and may withhold eligibility from any of them, by any criteria the licensor chooses.

6. **Permitted Endeavors**

The license may restrict the fields of endeavor or activities in which the program is used, by any criteria the licensor chooses.

7. **Distribution of License**

The rights attached to the program must apply to all eligible parties to whom the program is redistributed without the need for execution of an additional license by those parties. The original license determines eligibility.

8. **License May Be Specific to a Product**

The rights attached to the program may depend on the program being (or not being) part of a particular software distribution. 

9. **License May Restrict Other Software**

The license may place restrictions on other software that is distributed along with the licensed software. For example, the license may insist that all other programs distributed on the same medium must carry specific sovereign restrictions.

10. **License May Be Technology-specific**

The license may allow or restrict integration with any individual technology or technology family.

A license that imposes none of the permitted restrictions is still Sovereign. Open Source licenses are sovereign licenses. 

The licensor cannot arbitrarily revoke permissions granted. The licensee may exercise those permissions only while complying with, and remaining eligible under, the conditions of the license.

The Sovereign Software Definition was originally derived from the Debian Free Software Guidelines (DFSG) and Open Source Definition (OSD) but updated to understand that the expanded capability for bad actors to exploit licenses to do harm represents an existential threat to natural persons.
